Before you start (2 minutes, read once)

You are helping a research study on how well automated systems answer questions about long, multi-month text conversations between friends. The answer you grade will be consumed by an assistant acting on it: a good answer lets it interpret the situation correctly; a wrong or misleading one sends it the wrong way. Grade with that reader in mind.

All conversations took place in 2023. Relative time expressions (“last summer”, “next month”) count from the moment of the conversation, in 2023.

Each case shows a question, a gold answer (our reference), and a system answer. Your job: does the system answer convey the fact named in the gold answer?

  • The gold answer is your only measure of truth. You never need outside knowledge and cannot check the real world — don't try.
  • More or less detail than the gold is fine: if the gold fact is conveyed and nothing contradicts it, it's CORRECT. Paraphrase, partial recall of a list, reordering — fine. You are not asked to verify details the gold doesn't mention.
  • A different fact, a contradiction, or a missing fact — WRONG.
  • Genuinely can't tell whether the gold fact is there? AMBIGUOUS — a full verdict, not a failure. Use it freely.
  • Judge like a fair human reader, not a string matcher. There is no “right” share of CORRECT/WRONG — we don't know it ourselves.
