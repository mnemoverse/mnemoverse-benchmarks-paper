## P1

\path{kit/data/mem0_oss_answers/} (Mem0's published Platform answers, all ten conversations, Apache-2.0 from \texttt{mem0ai/memory-benchmarks@4b61c5d}---the commit our \texttt{mem0} prompt is ported from; the 92.5\% figure is recorded in that release and sourced in \path{kit/docs/competitor_claims.md}) re-scored by \path{kit/scripts/run_experiments.py}, with the scored aggregate committed at \path{experiments/hardening/summary.json} (key \texttt{B\_cross\_conversation}) and the per-answer verdicts at \path{experiments/hardening/verdicts/}. The overall 91.0\%/35.0\% is the $n$-weighted mean of the ten per-conversation cells (n-weighted overall gap 56.1\,pp); the per-conversation swing is 48.0--61.2\,pp with unweighted mean 56.2 (sd 4.0). $n=1539$ of the 1{,}540 upstream evaluations: one upstream row (\texttt{conv3\_q78}) carries an empty generated answer and an \texttt{ERROR} judgment and is excluded identically from both re-judges; the release covers LoCoMo's four non-adversarial categories (the adversarial category is not part of this upstream run). The two prompts disagree on 863 answers (counted by \path{scripts/count_disagreements.py} over those committed verdicts), and the disagreement is \emph{entirely one-directional}: on every one the lenient prompt credits an answer the strict prompt rejects (crediting ``last year'' for a gold of ``2022,'' or a date inside its $\pm$14-day tolerance), and the strict prompt never credits an answer the lenient one rejects. Eight of the 863 are the strict prompt being pedantic about formatting (``Sushi'' for a gold of ``sushi''); the remaining 855 were not hand-adjudicated---and the blind adjudication of the control slice (\S\ref{sec:judges-error}) warns against reading un-adjudicated disagreement as genuine leniency. One-directionality is partly by construction---the strict rubric is near-nested inside the lenient one---so the empirical content of the 863:0 is that judge-model noise never once violates the nesting in 863 opportunities; the magnitude is characterized per conversation and per category above. The ten conversations are the \texttt{locomo10} evaluation release the field's harnesses ship; the original LoCoMo corpus is larger (50 conversations\citep{locomo}). Neither prompt is ground truth; the swing measures the headline's \emph{sensitivity} to a choice the leaderboards never disclose.

## P2

\path{kit/data/variance_study_20260603_2300.json} (committed)---240 records (3 repeats $\times$ 20 qids $\times$ 4 judges). Two quantities live in this file and must not be conflated (an earlier revision of the paper conflated them). The \emph{per-question} verdict standard deviation across repeats is $\approx$0.014 (the per-judge \texttt{mean\_within\_qid\_stdev} is 0.029 for the two \texttt{gpt-5} judges and 0 for the others; averaged across the 80 question$\times$judge cells, $\approx$0.014). That is the noise on a \emph{single verdict}, NOT on a score: re-running each judge's 20-question mean across repeats and rescaling to a 152-question slice gives $\approx$0.52\,pp (recomputed by \path{scripts/noise_band.py}; sample sd throughout, the convention this file's own \texttt{stdev} field uses). The between-judge standard deviation across the four judge means is $\approx$0.107 = 10.7\,pp---a spread of \emph{means}, comparable to the slice-level 0.52\,pp figure and twenty-odd times larger than it, but not to the per-question 0.014. That study runs on the conv-26 / \texttt{mnemoverse\_engine} cell; a direct check on the load-bearing q8 set---re-judged three times under each prompt (\path{kit/scripts/run_experiments.py})---confirms it: the \texttt{mem0} score is identical to four decimals on all three repeats (0.7413) and \texttt{strict} varies by only $\approx$0.02, so the prompt-to-prompt swing is 41.3 / 40.6 / 39.9\,pp (sd 0.57\,pp), more than an order of magnitude below the effect ($\approx$70$\times$). The summary roll-up of that study is flagged stale for its cell numbers (pre-reranker), but the within-$\ll$-between ordering holds.

## P3

\path{experiments/golden_judge/FULLRUN_GOLDEN_RESULTS.json} + per-conversation verdicts in \path{experiments/golden_judge/fullrun_verdicts/}. Five of the 1{,}539 answers returned unparseable judge output; on the 1{,}534 jointly-scored answers the lenient prompt scores 91.0\% and the calibrated judge 85.3\%, an inflation of 5.7\,pp. The estimate is robust to every handling: keeping all 1{,}539 in the lenient aggregate gives 5.8; counting the five unparseable as incorrect gives 6.0---so 5.7--6.0, and we say ``about six'' in prose. Excluding conversation 26 entirely (its \emph{questions}, though not these Platform answers, appeared in the judge's calibration signal via the control slice) leaves it at 5.6--5.7 depending on handling. [The headline 5.7 is the joint-denominator value on all ten conversations (n=1534). Excluding conversation 26 (its questions fed the judge's calibration signal via the control slice) it is 5.64 (n=1383), computed by `scripts/per_conv_calibrated.py`.] The full-run re-judge is a single repeat per prompt; the three-repeat variance measurement on the control slice (swing sd 0.57\,pp, \S\ref{sec:threats}) bounds the judge noise far below the effect.

## P4

\path{kit/data/RECALL_AT_K_SUMMARY.json}---naked-cosine overall recall@k on LoCoMo conv-26 (n = 150 evidence-bearing questions of the 152-question slice): 0.595 / 0.721 / 0.780 / 0.886 / 0.938 / 0.981 at k = 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 / 100 / 200; multi-hop 0.242 at k = 5. \path{kit/scripts/recompute_recall.py} recomputes naked-cosine recall bit-for-bit from the retrieved and gold ids and certifies it \texttt{exact}, but flags the learned engine's recall \texttt{n\_a}---consolidated atoms lack a guaranteed one-to-one map to gold-evidence ids---so we anchor on the naked floor and do not cite an engine-versus-naked recall delta. Both systems embed with OpenAI \texttt{text-embedding-3-small}; naked cosine is embed + store + cosine-top-k with no learned memory, the saturation calibrator we adopt as a recall floor.

## P6

The adjudication covers all 57 lenient/strict disagreements of the control slice and all 64 exhibit disagreements of the engine answer set; integrity checks confirm every labeled case is a genuine disagreement with the recorded direction. The annotator is the author---a conflict we disclose rather than hide; the labeling was blind to machine verdicts, and the labels are committed with the paper's artifacts (\path{kit/judge_audit/human_labels_control_slice.json}, \path{kit/judge_audit/human_labels_engine_side.json}). An out-of-sample validation set is reported below; a relabeling campaign by additional annotators is underway (\S\ref{sec:threats}).
