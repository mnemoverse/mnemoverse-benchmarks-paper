# Contributing citation or wording fixes

Rules for proposing edits to `paper/current/main.tex` or `paper/refs.bib`, by hand or with a
coding agent. Numbers, tables, figures and artifact references are regenerated from committed
artifacts and are never edited by hand; releases (tags, bundles) are cut by the maintainers.

Paste the block below into Claude Code (or any coding agent) from a clone of this repo,
replacing the bracketed part with the citation or wording/style changes you want. The agent
does the edit, verifies the build, and opens a pull request. You never compile or freeze
anything — the lead author assembles and freezes from git.

**One thing to know before a style pass:** this paper went through three review rounds that
calibrated specific hedges word by word — e.g. "can dominate" (not "dominates"), "does not
resolve on this slice" (not "is not a ranking"), "plug-in estimate" (not "model contrast"),
"localization, not mechanism". A stylistic rephrase can quietly strengthen a claim past what
the evidence supports. That is fine to propose — just flag any reword that changes what a
sentence *asserts*, so the lead author can check it against the artifacts. Polish freely;
call out meaning changes.

---

```text
You are editing the paper "The Judge Is the Benchmark" in this repo. I want to make
citation and/or wording (style) edits only.

WHAT I WANT CHANGED:
[ describe the edits in plain words — citations (e.g. "cite the journal version of Mem0,
  DOI 10.xxxx/yyyy"; paste DOIs / arXiv IDs / URLs for anything new) and/or wording
  (e.g. "tighten the second paragraph of §3", "the abstract reads clunky, smooth it"). ]

RULES — follow exactly:
1. Edit paper/current/main.tex (prose + \cite/\citep) and paper/refs.bib. Do not touch any
   other file, and do not touch tables, figures, or \path{...} artifact references.
2. Do NOT change any number, statistic, table cell, or claim. Every number in this paper
   traces to a committed artifact and is regenerated by scripts; editing one by hand breaks
   that. If a citation edit seems to require changing a number, STOP and tell me instead.
3. Do NOT create release tags or freeze a new PDF. The lead author does the release.
   For any REWORD, keep the claim's strength: this paper spent three review rounds
   calibrating its hedges. If a smoother sentence would assert more (or less) than the
   original, keep the original and note the proposed change in the PR instead.
   Keep the abstract under 1920 characters (arXiv limit); it is currently near it.
4. Every bib entry must trace to a primary source. For any entry you add or change, put the
   DOI / arXiv ID / official URL in the entry and confirm the metadata (authors, title, year,
   pages) against that source. A wrong DOI is exactly the kind of untraceable number this
   paper is about. If you cannot verify a source, leave it and flag it.

THEN:
5. Verify the build still compiles cleanly:
      bash paper/current/build.sh
   It must print "BUILD OK" with no undefined citations or references. Fix your own edit if
   it does not.
6. Open a pull request against main with a short title like "citations: <what you changed>"
   and, in the body, list each entry you changed with the source you verified it against.
   Do not merge — the lead author reviews and merges.
```

---

Prefer to do it by hand? Even simpler: edit `paper/refs.bib` (and any `\citep` in
`paper/current/main.tex`), run `bash paper/current/build.sh` to confirm it still says
`BUILD OK`, and open a pull request. No need to build figures or freeze anything.
